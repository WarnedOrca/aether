# 10-minute conversation guide

Goal: learn about **one** task the business repeats by hand, well enough to describe it back in one sentence and to record an honest "before" estimate. You are not selling anything and not promising a result.

Before you go: a parent or guardian knows about this conversation. Bring a notebook. Do not ask for, write down or photograph any customer names, phone numbers or other customer details.

## Opening (1 minute)

> Thanks for making time. I'm [your name], a student at [school]. I'm working on a free project that tries to take one repetitive task off a small business owner's plate, with the owner approving anything before it goes to a customer. Today I just want to understand how one task works for you. It's about ten minutes, and I don't need any customer information. Is it okay if I take notes?

If they say no to notes, ask whether you can write a summary afterwards instead.

## Questions (7 minutes)

1. **The task.** "What's one thing you or your staff do over and over by hand that you'd happily stop doing?" If they list several, ask: "Which one costs you the most time or causes the most trouble?" Stay with that one.
2. **Walk-through.** "Could you walk me through the last time you did it, step by step?" Listen for: what starts it, where the information lives (paper, spreadsheet, booking app, texts), what gets sent or changed, and to whom.
3. **How often and how long.** "Roughly how many times a week does that happen? And about how long does it take each time?" Write their words down exactly and mark them as **their estimate**.
4. **What goes wrong.** "What happens when it gets rushed or forgotten? Has anything gone wrong because of it?" If they mention missed or late messages, ask how often, roughly, and mark that as an estimate too.
5. **Who it affects.** "Who notices when it goes wrong: you, your staff, customers?"
6. **Rules.** "If someone else did this for you, what should they never do without asking you first? Is there anything a customer must never see, or any money limit?"
7. **Approval.** "Would you want to approve every message yourself, or check a batch at once?"

## Closing (2 minutes)

- Say the task back in one sentence: "So the task is [trigger] → [what gets sent or changed] → [to whom], about [their estimate]. Is that right?"
- Be clear about where the project is: "Right now my tool can show you exactly what it would do, using made-up sample data. It doesn't connect to your systems yet, and nothing would ever go to a customer without your approval."
- Ask: "Would it be okay if I came back in about a week with a written description of what it would do, for you to check?" Accept "no" or "maybe later" with thanks.

## Right after (your notes, kept in `private/`, never committed)

- The one task, in one sentence.
- Their estimates: times per week, minutes each time, problems per week. Copy them into `private/pilot-log.csv` as a **before** row with today's date and "owner's estimate" as the method.
- Their rules (who may be contacted, forbidden fields, limits, sending hours, approval preference).
- Red flags: health, legal, children's or payment data; anything that would mean moving money. Prefer a different task for a first pilot.
- What you promised them, if anything, and the date you said you would follow up.
