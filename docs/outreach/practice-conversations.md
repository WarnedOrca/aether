# Practice: three common objections

These are honest answers to adapt into your own words. Practise them out loud with a parent or friend. The rule for every answer: say what the project can do today, not what it might do someday, and never call it "safe".

## 1. "I'm too busy."

> That's fair, and it's kind of why I'm asking: the point is to take something off your plate, not add to it. I only need ten minutes, and I can come at whatever time is quietest for you, or just leave a one-page sheet and check back another week. If it's never a good time, that's completely fine too.

Then actually leave it. Don't ask more than twice.

## 2. "Is my customer data safe?"

> Good question. For the first part I don't need any of your customer data at all. I test everything on made-up names and numbers with the same columns as your sheet. If we ever get to using real data, that only happens if you agree in writing, you choose exactly which columns it can see, and you can ask me to delete everything at any time.
>
> I won't promise "safe", because I can't guarantee that about any software. What I can show you is narrower: the tool has a checker that refuses any program that would message a customer without your approval, read a column you didn't allow, or put a field you marked private into a message. In every test so far, on made-up test data, the programs the checker accepted caused zero violations of those rules.

Don't add more than that. If they want details, offer to show the plain-English description of the program.

## 3. "What's the catch?"

> Honestly, the catch is that it's an early student project. It's free, and I'm not selling anything. What I get is real experience and, if it works, something I can write about, but only with your permission, and your business isn't named unless you say yes.
>
> The other catch is that it's not finished. Right now it can show you exactly what it would do using test data, but it doesn't connect to your systems yet, so it won't save you time on day one. It can also make mistakes, which is why you approve everything.

If they ask "so why should I bother?", a fair answer is: "Only if this task really bugs you. If it doesn't, you've spent ten minutes helping a student, and I appreciate it."
